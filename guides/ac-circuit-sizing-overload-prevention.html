AC Circuit Sizing, Cable Selection & Thermal Overload Prevention in 50°C UAE Heat

By Ahammed Kabeer (Electrical & Maintenance Specialist) Updated September 2026 9 Min Read Designed for 50°C+ Ambient UAE Conditions

During the peak summer months across the UAE (Dubai, Sharjah, Abu Dhabi, and the Northern Emirates), ambient outdoor air temperatures routinely reach 45°C to 50°C, with direct solar radiation heating unshaded villa rooftops and external cable conduits to over 65°C. In this punishing climate, air conditioning systems account for up to 70% of a villa's total electrical power consumption. Electrical circuits that were sized casually without applying proper thermal de-rating factors suffer persistent nuisance tripping, melted terminal insulation, compressor burnouts, and dangerous electrical fires.

1. The Physics of Conductor Thermal De-Rating in the Arabian Gulf

Standard international cable tables (such as BS 7671 baseline charts) define the continuous current-carrying capacity ($I_z$) of PVC and XLPE copper conductors at a European standard ambient temperature of 30°C.

In the UAE, the baseline design ambient temperature enforced by DEWA and FEWA regulations is 40°C in shaded areas and 50°C or higher for outdoor and rooftop installations. As ambient temperature climbs, the temperature difference between the energized copper core and the surrounding air narrows, drastically reducing the rate at which heat can dissipate. To prevent the conductor insulation from exceeding its maximum continuous thermal rating (70°C for standard PVC, 90°C for thermosetting XLPE), the cable's current rating must be multiplied by an Ambient Temperature Correction Factor ($C_a$):

Ambient Operating Temperature PVC Conductor De-Rating Factor ($C_a$) XLPE Conductor De-Rating Factor ($C_a$) Practical Current Capacity Reduction
30°C (Standard European Baseline) 1.00 1.00 0% Reduction (Full Capacity)
40°C (UAE Indoor / Shaded) 0.87 0.91 13% Current Capacity Lost
45°C (UAE Typical Summer) 0.79 0.87 21% Current Capacity Lost
50°C (UAE Rooftop Outdoor Ambience) 0.71 0.82 29% Current Capacity Lost
55°C (Direct Sunlight Rooftop Conduit) 0.61 0.76 39% Current Capacity Lost
The Danger of Undersized 2.5mm² AC Cables

Many contractors in older UAE buildings mistakenly used $2.5\text{mm}^2$ copper cables for 2.0-ton and 2.5-ton AC units. While $2.5\text{mm}^2$ carries 24A at 30°C, at 50°C on a blazing rooftop its safe capacity drops to just 17A! A 2.0-ton compressor pulling 13A continuously with condenser fan heat will push the copper core beyond 75°C, turning the PVC insulation brittle and brittle, leading to direct copper shorts inside the ceiling.

3. Why Breaker Tripping Curve Matters: Type B vs. Type C MCBs

When an AC compressor starts up, it must overcome the stationary inertia of the pistons and high head refrigerant pressure in the copper lines. For approximately 100 to 300 milliseconds, the compressor motor draws its Locked Rotor Amps (LRA), which is typically $4\times$ to $6\times$ higher than its normal running Full Load Amps (FLA).

  • Type B MCB: Calibrated to trip magnetically at $3\times$ to $5\times$ rated current. Using a Type B breaker on an AC compressor will result in frequent nuisance trips on hot afternoons when compressor head pressure is at maximum.
  • Type C MCB (Mandatory for HVAC): Calibrated to withstand inductive motor inrush currents of $5\times$ to $10\times$ rated current without tripping, while still providing instantaneous protection against genuine short circuits.

4. Rooftop Weatherproof Rotary Isolator Switches

Under UAE civil safety and utility regulations, an outdoor condensing unit mounted on a villa roof or external yard must have a dedicated, locked rotary isolator switch located within 1.5 meters line-of-sight of the unit.

IP65 / IP66 Ingress Protection

Isolator enclosures must be sealed against high-pressure windblown dust and torrential winter rain. Never install indoor-rated toggle switches outdoors.

UV-Stabilized Polycarbonate Casing

Intense UAE ultraviolet radiation destroys cheap plastic enclosures within 18 months, causing the rotary handle to crack and break. High-grade UV-resistant enclosures prevent water ingress.

Annual Terminal Retorquing

Due to severe daily thermal expansion (30°C at night to 50°C+ at noon), screw terminals inside isolators loosen over time. Loose screw terminals create micro-arcing that melts the switch base, frequently misdiagnosed as a compressor failure!

5. Diagnostic Checklist When Your AC Keeps Tripping the Breaker

  1. Does it trip instantly when the thermostat clicks? Indicates a dead short circuit in the contactor, a grounded compressor terminal, or a melted run capacitor.
  2. Does it trip after running for 10 to 30 minutes? Indicates thermal overload caused by an undersized MCB, a blocked/dirty condenser coil causing extreme head pressure, or high ambient terminal resistance.
  3. Does it trip the main RCCB (30mA) instead of the branch MCB? Indicates an internal motor winding breakdown where current is leaking through refrigerant oil to the compressor grounded metal shell.
