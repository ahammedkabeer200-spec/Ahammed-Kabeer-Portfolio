Faulty Appliance / Local Short
The appliance you are turning on likely has a internal short circuit or ground leakage.
  • Action: Unplug the appliance immediately.
  • Reset the tripped breaker switch in your main distribution board.
  • If the breaker stays ON, the fault is isolated to the appliance. Avoid plugging it back in.
Potential Earth Leakage / Cable Fault
This random tripping is commonly caused by moisture seepages, rodent-damaged cables, or a failing ELCB (Earth Leakage Circuit Breaker).
  • Warning: Earth leakages pose a high risk of electric shock and cable fires.
  • A Megger insulation resistance test is required to locate the weak point.
  • Contact Ahammed immediately to test your DB and wiring safely.
Sub-Circuit Overloading
Your breaker is tripping because the combined current of multiple active heavy appliances exceeds the circuit breaker rating (typically 16A or 20A for household sockets).
  • Action: Turn off one of the high-power appliances (e.g. run AC first, then heater).
  • Have an electrician balance the circuits in the panel, or lay down a dedicated circuit line for the heavy appliances.
Fixture / Lamp Failure
Flickering on a single fixture typically indicates a dying LED driver, loose bulb contacts inside the holder, or a faulty dimmer switch.
  • Action: Turn off the power switch, wait for bulb to cool down, and check if it is screwed in tight.
  • Try replacing the bulb with a new one to verify.
Severe Loose Neutral Warning
Flickering across multiple circuits is an emergency indicator of a loose neutral connection in your main distribution board. This can cause severe voltage spikes up to 400V, frying home electronics and starting panel fires.
  • Warning: Turn off your main breaker immediately if lights are flashing intensely.
  • Do not attempt to tighten distribution board connections yourself.
  • Contact Ahammed immediately to check neutral terminations.
Damaged Outlet Outlet / loose contact
A single dead outlet is usually caused by terminal screws loosening behind the plate, or local heat damage (overheating from heavy load).
  • Warning: Loose wires inside the wall box can spark and create electrical fires.
  • Avoid using this outlet entirely.
  • Contact Ahammed to safely uninstall the outlet, check wire integrity, and mount a replacement faceplate.
Tripped Sub-Circuit or Broken Line
When multiple outlets go dead together, they are likely on the same radial or ring circuit which has either tripped at the DB panel or suffered a cable break.
  • Check your distribution board. Reset any switch that is in the OFF/tripped position.
  • If the breaker trips back down immediately, do not force it. Contact Ahammed to trace the line short.
Burned Element or Faulty Thermostat
Since power is reaching the water heater (switch indicator is ON) but water is cold, the heating element inside the tank has likely burned out or the thermostat safety cut-off has triggered.
  • Action: Keep the heater switch OFF to save energy and protect the tank.
  • Contact Ahammed to drain the heater, test the element continuity, and replace the thermostat/heating coil.
Power Delivery Failure
No power is reaching the heater unit. The double-pole switch neon light is off, pointing to a tripped breaker in the distribution board or burned contacts inside the double-pole isolator switch.
  • Check the dedicated heater breaker switch in the main board.
  • If the board breaker is ON but the switch light is OFF, the switch unit itself needs testing/replacement.

Voltage Fluctuations / Surges
Frequent voltage surges can damage expensive electronics (AC compressors, TVs, fridges). This can be caused by external grid fluctuations or heavy motors turning on inside the house.
  • Action: Unplug sensitive electronic devices during severe fluctuations.
  • Contact Ahammed to install a whole-house Surge Protection Device (SPD) in your distribution board to shield your home!
Current Leakage or Inefficient Loads
Sudden electricity bill increases are often caused by resistive leakages to earth (current slowly draining to ground through wet walls or damaged insulation) or aging AC compressors pulling excessive current.
  • Action: Turn off all breakers; if the main meter continues spinning rapidly, you have a line leakage.
  • Contact Ahammed to run a comprehensive insulation resistance check (Megger test) to identify and isolate current leaks.
Switch Arcing / Loose Terminals
Sparks seen inside a switch casing indicate that the contacts are loose or eroded, creating a mini electric arc when bridging the connection.
  • Warning: Continuous arcing generates heat that eventually melts the switch plastic and creates a fire risk.
  • Stop using the switch. Keep it in the OFF position.
  • Have Ahammed replace the switch unit with a quality replacement.
EMERGENCY: Overloaded / Melting Cables
A burning plastic smell is a critical warning sign that wire insulation is melting due to high resistance loose connections or extreme circuit overloading.
  • Warning: This is a major fire hazard! Switch OFF the main breaker for that circuit immediately.
  • Do not touch hot outlets or wires.
  • Contact Ahammed immediately to trace the melting wires and replace damaged conductors.
